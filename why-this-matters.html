The full picture

Why this
matters now.

The landscape of work and education is shifting faster than most schools or parents can track. Here is what we think parents need to understand, and why we started Arslan Institute to do something about it.

The problem

Every parent is asking the same question.

You've done everything right. Good schools, tutoring when needed, music lessons, sports, weekends spent shuttling between activities. But underneath it all, there's a quieter worry. The world your teenager is growing into doesn't look like the one you prepared for. Computer science graduates are struggling to find junior roles. AI is rewriting what entry-level work means. The safe bets don't feel safe anymore.

Most after-school activities are still optimised for a world that's already changing. What teenagers actually need now is something different. Not more coding clubs. Not abstract lectures about AI. A real, working understanding of how to build with the most powerful technology of their lifetime, before it becomes table stakes.

The argument

The people who will thrive aren't the ones who can code.

They're the ones who deeply understand a domain, medicine, design, business, science, journalism, and can build with AI to multiply themselves. Domain expertise plus AI fluency. That combination is becoming the single most valuable skill across almost every field.

Teenagers are uniquely placed to learn this. They already use AI daily. What they're missing is the bridge between using it as a homework shortcut and using it as a creative, productive tool. That's what we teach.

The most successful students will be those who use AI to help make conceptual connections for developing ideas. Students who learn to use AI ethically and productively may learn at an exponentially higher rate than others, in a way that allows them to remain competitive throughout their careers.

Sal Khan, Founder of Khan Academy, author of Brave New Words

The obvious tactical thing is just get really good at using AI tools. When I was graduating as a senior from high school, the obvious tactical thing was get really good at coding, and this is the new version of that.

Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI

The hottest new programming language is English.

Andrej Karpathy, Co-founder of OpenAI
